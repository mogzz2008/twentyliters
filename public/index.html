See a household day in twenty-liter cans.

A 20-liter jerrycan is the container millions of people still carry when water is not on the premises. Twenty Liters translates a household’s daily water into cans, kilograms, and walking hours — using published figures from EPA WaterSense, USGS, the Water Research Foundation, WHO, and the WHO/UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme. Nothing here is invented. Every number has a source.

Sources and limits

Twenty Liters is an educational comparison tool, not a utility bill, engineering study, or statement about any named community’s actual walk.

  1. EPA WaterSense, How We Use Water — the average American family uses more than 300 gallons of water per day at home; roughly 70% indoors, 30% outdoors nationally, and outdoor use “can be as much as 60 percent of total household water use in arid regions.”
  2. EPA WaterSense, Statistics and Facts — each American uses an average of 82 gallons a day at home, citing USGS, Estimated Use of Water in the United States in 2015.
  3. EPA WaterSense, Showerheads — standard showerheads use 2.5 gallons per minute; WaterSense labeled heads use no more than 2.0 gpm.
  4. EPA WaterSense, Residential Toilets — WaterSense toilets use 1.28 gallons per flush or less, 20% below the federal standard of 1.6 gpf.
  5. EPA WaterSense, Bathroom Faucets — WaterSense bathroom faucets and accessories use a maximum of 1.5 gpm, versus a standard flow of 2.2 gpm.
  6. EPA WaterSense, Fix a Leak Week — “A leaky faucet that drips at the rate of one drip per second can waste more than 3,000 gallons per year.” Average-family leak totals (180 gallons per week / 9,400 gallons per year) are cited on the Statistics and Facts page.
  7. DeOreo, W.B., et al., Residential End Uses of Water, Version 2 (Water Research Foundation, 2016) — indoor use of 138 gallons per household per day, with toilets 33.1, faucets 28.1, showers 26.3, clothes washers 22.7, leaks 17.0, other 5.3, baths 3.6, and dishwashers 1.6 gphd. The published total rounds the component sum of 137.7 gallons.
  8. Howard, G. and Bartram, J., Domestic Water Quantity, Service Level and Health (WHO, 2003) — typical quantities of about 5, 20, 50, and 100–200 liters per person per day associated with no, basic, intermediate, and optimal access.
  9. WHO/UNICEF JMP, Progress on household drinking water, sanitation and hygiene 2000–2024 (August 2025) — 74% (about 6 billion people) used a safely managed drinking-water service in 2024; 2.1 billion still lacked one, including 1.4 billion with basic services, 287 million limited, 302 million unimproved, and 106 million using surface water.
  10. WHO/UNICEF JMP gender analysis, 2023 — 1.8 billion people lived in households without water on premises, and women and girls aged 15+ were primarily responsible for collection in 7 out of 10 of those households.

Laundry and dishwasher gallons in the fixture lab are editable assumptions, shown so you can compare machines, and are not labeled as EPA WaterSense product standards. Water mass uses the 1 liter ≈ 1 kilogram convention. All math runs in your browser. There is no account and no tracking.
